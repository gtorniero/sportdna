Common mistakes when buying a racket

Buying your favorite pro's racket

Tour models carry more weight and swingweight. If you play twice a week, you end up late on the ball and your arm tires out.

Assuming lighter means more comfortable

A racket that's too light tends to send more shock to the elbow. Low stiffness with enough mass is usually more comfortable. No racket prevents or treats an injury.

Getting the grip size wrong

A handle that fits your hand lets you hold it without squeezing too hard, which is usually more comfortable. A handle that's too big locks up your wrist.

Stiff poly, high tension, overdue restring

A combo that tends to be rough on the elbow. Strings go dead long before they break.

Going oversize for “forgiveness”

A bigger head also means more pop. If you already have a long swing, the ball sails long.

Trusting the printed specs

The same model can vary by up to 7 g from frame to frame, and swingweight isn't even listed. Our swingweight and stiffness numbers come from independent measurements.
